== Defining Bias

Bias is *a systematic error in data or modeling that leads to distorted representations of reality*

Bias comes from many sources:

* data bias
** prejudiced/incorrect collection
* historical bias
** old outdated policies
* labeling bias
** prejudiced/incorrect collection

Bias has a certain structure:

* not random => *systematic*
** i.e. not noise
* structured and directional
* affects some groups more than others
** errors are not evenly distributed
** certain populations are consistently misrepresented

=== Formal perspective

Given:

* X: features
* Y: ground truth
* Y': predicted outcome

Bias can be seen as:

* Y != Y' systematically for some subgroup
* or dataset P(X, Y) not matching its corresponding real world distribution for some subgroup

Concerns data and learning

== Defining Fairness

Fairness is the *normative property of a system*

* kind of foggy
* concerns outcomes and impact

A *fair* system ensures equitable outcomes across sensitive groups

CAUTION: Fairness is not guaranteed even with unbiased data

Fairness is not directly observable

* measured through metrics
* different metrics measure different aspects
* can compare model behavior across groups defined by sensitive features
** e.g. sex, ethnicity
** requires sensitive features, ground truth, predictions

== Fairness and Bias

If data is biased the model will be biased

Data can be biased for lots of reasons:

* collection methods
* collection population
* data handling/preprocessing

Also for socioeconomic reasons

* income differences preventing access to certain resources

...and historical reasons

* inherited inequalities
* outdated guidelines

...and geographical reasons

* urban vs rural
* language barriers
* facilities/resources exist only in other locations
** have to travel

Fairness is pervasive and very important to consider

* digital systems can very easily inherit all these biases
** need to design them very carefully

If the training data of a model is heavily unbalanced it will cause poor performance for the underrepresented group

* this performance will be  *systematically* worse

=== Fairness in digital systems

DSs can:

* amplify (not generate) biases
* formalize inequalities
* hide biasess/inequalities

Mitigating impact of biases/inequalities needs to be formalized

* what do we need to take into account?
* how does this algorithm need to be designed?
** e.g. get similar results for feature parity except bias feature (e.g. detect skin cancer in equal samples with different color)
* how does this statistical evaluation need to be implemented?

Not mitigating biases/inequalities can cause problems

* bad diagnoses
* bad treatment recommendations
* unfair resource allocation
* bias amplification
* bias feedback loops

=== Addressing Bias and Fairness

There are legal and ethical implications:

* GDPR/AI act violations
** GDPR has regulations against discrimination
* professional liability
** if a decision made by an algorithm is bad the doctor becomes responsible
* patient safety
** if a decision made by an algorithm is bad the patient might suffer

There are also domain specific challenges:

* biological differences
* biased datasets
* limited ground truth

Which affect model robustness

* biased models -> poor generalizability -> bad performance

Sometimes bias can be corrected after data correction

* dataset rebalancing
** performance on some metric (e.g. accuracy) may improve for the underrepresented class

...but that does not necessarily remove *unfairness*

* another metric may still be systematically worse (e.g. False Negative Rate)

== Societal bias to algorithmic bias

Digital systems will inherit any bias found in data collected from society from the usual suspects

* historical bias
** may even be invisible in the data
** may be due to some other policies that affect the data generation process
** or historical/cultural artefacts
* representation bias
** collecting data from a specific population only/with an overwhelming majority
* measurement bias
** measuring a variable and including it as a feature may cause bias

Bias can come up in modeling as well:

* evaluation bias
** if eval/benchmark set is biased the results will not be representative of the general population
* aggregation bias
** aggregating data without considering the source populations can cause problems
*** e.g. merging BMI of adults with BMI of children
* deployment bias
** end user interpretation can cause perceived bias
** real world interactions can also cause new biases
